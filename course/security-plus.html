Module 3 · CompTIA Security+ (SY0 series)

Security+ fundamentals

Security+ is the first security cert most employers recognize. CYBS students get a voucher after CYB349. It is broader than Network+: people, process, and crypto, not only cables.

1. CIA triad

  • Confidentiality — only the right people see it (encryption, permissions, MFA).
  • Integrity — it was not secretly changed (hashes, signatures, backups).
  • Availability — it is there when needed (redundancy, patches, DDoS defense).

AAA: Authentication (who you are), Authorization (what you may do), Accounting (what you did — logs).

Practice. Ransomware encrypts files so the office cannot work. Which CIA letter is hit first?
Availability (and often integrity). Confidentiality may also be at risk if data was stolen.

2. Threat vs vulnerability vs risk vs control

TermMeaningExample
Threatsomething that can cause harmphishing email
Vulnerabilityweakness that can be usedunpatched VPN, reused password
Riskchance × impactlikely phish + access to payroll
Controlsomething you do about itMFA, training, filtering

Controls are preventive, detective, or corrective. A lock is preventive. A camera is detective. A restore from backup is corrective.

3. People attacks (social engineering)

  • Phishing — fake email. Spear phishing — targeted. Whaling — executives.
  • Vishing / smishing — voice / SMS.
  • Pretexting — fake story (“I’m from IT…”).
  • Tailgating — follow someone through a door.

Defense: training, reporting button, never give passwords on the phone, verify out-of-band.

4. Malware types

Virus needs a host file. Worm spreads by itself. Trojan pretends to be useful. Ransomware encrypts for payment. Spyware / keyloggers steal. Rootkits hide. Know the names; the exam loves definitions.

5. Crypto you must not mix up

IdeaReversible?Use
Encryptionyes, with a keykeep secrets
Hashingnointegrity, password storage
Encoding (Base64)yes, no secrettransport, not security
Digital signaturehash + private keyprove who sent it + not changed

Symmetric (AES): one shared key, fast. Asymmetric (RSA, ECC): public/private pair, used to share keys and sign. TLS uses both.

Practice. Should you store passwords with reversible encryption or with a hash + salt?
Hash + salt (and a slow algorithm). If stolen, attackers should not get the real passwords easily.

6. Identity and access

Least privilege: give the minimum access needed. MFA: something you know + have + are. SSO: one login to many apps. Privileged accounts (admin/root) get extra monitoring.

Zero Trust: never trust the network location alone. Verify every request.

7. Network security (ties to your lab)

  • Firewall: allow/deny ports and IPs.
  • VPN: encrypted tunnel (your reverse proxy is not a VPN).
  • Segmentation: VLANs, separate guest Wi-Fi, jump box (your Ubuntu SSH jump is this idea).
  • Disable unused services. Your Nginx VM had firewalld stopped for a lab — that is a tradeoff you must understand for the exam: availability vs security.

8. Incident response (simple order)

  1. Preparation
  2. Identification
  3. Containment
  4. Eradication
  5. Recovery
  6. Lessons learned

Do not power-wash evidence before you know policy. Document times and actions.

Practice. You confirm malware on one PC. What is containment?
Stop spread: isolate the host from the network, disable the account if needed, keep evidence. Do not immediately reimage if you still need logs — unless policy says otherwise.

Study order for the voucher

Finish Network+ ideas first if IP/ports still feel fuzzy. Then Security+. Official objectives: CompTIA Security+. After Security+, CySA+ is the analyst exam (CYBS second voucher).
